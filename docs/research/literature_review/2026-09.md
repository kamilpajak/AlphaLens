## Anti-pattern flags

Below are papers or themes that *look* tempting for alpha but resemble earlier failure modes or violate your triage rules in ways that would almost certainly re‑create past problems.

### 1. Retail order flow + patient limit orders (microstructure for retail)

**Retail Limit Orders — Anand, Samadi, Sokobin, Venkataraman (Review of Finance, OA)**[47][50]  
- **Looks enticing:** Large‑scale, regulatory‑grade dataset (27M orders from 19 retail brokers) shows **retail limit orders account for ~25–30% of orders/shares and deliver ≈10 bps lower implementation shortfall than marketable orders**, with fill rates ≈65% overall and ≈60% even in small caps.[47][49][50][57]  
- **Why it fails triage / anti‑pattern risk:**
  - **Filter 1 (sample ≥15y): fail.** The dataset is May 2020 only, i.e., ~1 month of a *high‑volatility, meme‑era* regime[47][49]. This is textbook single‑window overfit risk, akin to your small‑cap momentum overfitting.
  - **Filter 3 (public data): fail.** Uses FINRA OATS order‑level flow and broker IDs, unattainable for a retail quant[49][50].
  - **No net strategy with **<100% turnover**; paper decomposes costs across order types, but does not present an implementable cross‑stock alpha with net trading costs.
- **Use case:** Design/execution guidance if you trade anyway (lean to limit orders; expect ~10–20 bps cost improvement in small caps).[47][49][50][57]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** Temptation to translate “patient limit orders cheaper than market orders” into a *signal* (e.g., “stocks with high patient retail limit flow outperform”). Given single‑month data and proprietary flow, this would likely replicate your **Layer 2b / universe‑concentration overfit** problem.

**Payment for Order Flow and Option Internalization (RFS, 2026)**[60]  
- Option wholesalers internalize **retail option flow** via auctions and books, focusing on price improvement and internalization.[60]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** It invites “track PFOF or internalization metrics as an equity factor,” but the underlying data are proprietary (wholesaler/order‑routing detail) and the relation to stock‑level returns is indirect. Pursuing it risks building a pseudo‑alt‑data factor that **fails Filter 3 (public data)** and **resembles your crowded alt‑data Form 4 cluster buy** failure.

**A Unified Theory of Order Flow, Market Impact, and Volatility (q‑fin.ST, 2026)**[48]  
- Framework paper; attractive as a base for execution‑aware signals, but from the snippet it is theoretical/diagnostic rather than a low‑turnover cross‑sectional equity strategy with net returns[48].  
- **Anti‑pattern:** Very easy to use such theory to justify *short‑horizon microstructure alphas* that require tick‑level LOB data and high turnover, violating your turnover and cost filters.

### 2. LLM-driven analysis of 10‑K intangibles / risk disclosures

You asked explicitly to **exclude 8‑K event driven** strategies; one of your closed paradigms (Layer 2f) already shows median CAR −100 to −250 bps on 8‑K‑based trades. Nothing in 2024+ gives a clean, retail‑replicable 10‑K‑based alpha with proper OOS and multiple‑testing correction.

**Taxonomy‑Aligned Risk Extraction from 10‑K Filings with Autonomous Improvement Using LLMs (2026)**[16][19]  
- Uses an LLM pipeline (quote‑grounded extraction, embedding‑based mapping, LLM‑as‑judge) to extract **10,688 risk factors** from S&P 500 10‑Ks and builds taxonomy‑aligned risk profiles. Same‑industry firms have substantially more similar risk profiles than cross‑industry, with strong statistical validation (AUC 0.82, p<0.001).[16]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** Very tempting to “LLM‑score risk disclosure quality and long cheap / short rich on that signal” in a **Buffett‑style value overlay**, exactly your Layer 2g failure mode (LLM‑researcher picks, value bias). The paper is *diagnostic*, not a trading study:
  - No net returns, no turnover metrics.
  - No explicit multiple‑testing correction for any alpha.
  - No evidence that the risk‑taxonomy distance has robust post‑2020 predictive power for returns.
- Best role: feature engineering for *explanatory* models (risk similarity, governance quality), not a standalone factor.

**How Much of a 10‑K Matters? Aggregation‑ and Section‑Level Sentiment (2607.14174, 2026)**[30]  
- Extends supervised lexicon learning to 10‑K filings and Item 1A, training sentiment scores against return and volatility labels at multiple aggregation levels (sector, portfolio, firm).[30]  
- Finds that full‑filing sentiment works better at sector/portfolio level, but **Item 1A risk‑section sentiment works better at the individual‑firm level**, and that **Loughran‑McDonald dictionary sentiment is *negatively* correlated with price**, suggesting naive dictionary factors are mis‑signed.[30]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** Another strong temptation to construct a **single text‑sentiment factor**, backtest 2006–2023, then deploy on 2024+:
  - From the snippet we do not see a complete **OOS after 2020** with proper multiple‑test correction; we see supervised learning tuned on the same window that generates labels.
  - No explicit **net‑of‑cost, low‑turnover portfolio**; mapping high‑frequency predictive labels into a tradable long/short may require high turnover.
  - The improved model is still a *sentiment* feature that risks re‑creating **LLM‑researcher value nostalgia** if combined with fundamental screens.

**Agentic Framework for Financial Disclosure Intelligence (2026 TREOS paper)**[25]  
- Proposes an *agentic* ensemble of lexicon‑based and transformer models to score 10‑K Item 1A/Item 7 tone for 55 large‑cap firms, 2016–2025, shows directional agreement but differing sensitivity between Loughran‑McDonald and FinBERT.[25]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** Purely a measurement framework; no convincingly OOS tested, low‑turnover alpha. Easy to over‑fit long‑horizon cross‑sectional “sentiment value” factors and re‑enact your **value underperformance** problem.

**A Decade of S&P 500 10‑K Filings, 2015–2025 (Marin et al.)**[17][26]  
- Describes the **explosion in AI‑related risk disclosures**: S&P 500 AI risk mentions move from 17% of firms (2022) to 61% (2024) and 77.6% (2025).[17]  
- Again, no alpha; but the practitioner temptation is to invest in “firms that disclose AI risk more carefully or more extensively” — this would likely be *another crowd‑ed value/growth narrative bet* with no clean post‑2020 OOS validation.

### 3. Cross-asset overlays (commodity / FX overlays on equity factors)

**A TVP‑VAR analysis and portfolio hedging strategies … (equities, bonds, energy, metals, crypto, agri)**[37]  
- Uses **TVP‑VAR** to assess dynamic connectedness of equities, bonds, energy, metals, crypto and agricultural commodities from 2020–02 to 2024–07, then applies findings to portfolios using minimum variance (MVP), minimum correlation (MCP), and minimum connectedness (MCoP) strategies.[37]  
- Main result: cross‑asset connectedness is time‑varying; MCP and MCoP hedging deliver higher Sharpe than MVP when equity‑bond correlation is high.[37]  
- **Anti‑pattern:**  
  - This is **portfolio optimization**, not an equity factor overlay; no explicit test that, say, commodity‑equity connectedness scores overlay on *equity factors* (e.g., momentum, quality).  
  - No rigorous multiple‑testing correction, and asset coverage is limited; if you fit overlay rules on 2020–2024, you risk **single‑regime overfit** like your tactical sector rotation layer (R²≈0.999 vs passive).  
  - Implementing a similar TVP‑VAR overlay in retail context would require *dense cross‑asset futures data* and stable parameter estimation — structurally fragile.

**Deep Learning for Financial Time Series: A Large-Scale Benchmark (2603.01820, 2026)**[44]  
- Benchmarks deep architectures for Sharpe optimization on daily futures spanning commodities, equity indices, bonds, and FX from 2010–2025, focusing on **Sharpe‑maximizing trading rules**.[44]  
- Attractive because of full post‑2010 data and cross‑asset coverage, but:  
  - The snippet does not show **net returns including realistic transaction costs**; when deep models trade daily on multiple futures, turnover is likely high.  
  - No evidence of **multiple‑testing correction** across architectures, hyperparameters and signal combinations.  
- **Anti‑pattern:** A strong candidate to replicate your **tactical rotation / regime‑switch** failure: choose the best Sharpe model from a benchmark suite, mistake a multiple‑tested winner for a robust strategy.

**Energy Market and Carbon Emission Spillovers in Critical … (2607.27485)**[42]  
- Explores spillovers between energy and carbon markets; interesting for macro‑thematic overlays but no clear path to **low‑turnover equity factor overlays** with net‑of‑cost performance.

### 4. Factor decay 2025+ with OOS validation post‑2020

This is where most of the genuinely useful *calibration* work is, but it largely **disconfirms** easy reactivation stories.

**Not All Factors Crowd Equally: Modeling, Measuring, and Trading on Alpha Decay (2512.11913, 2025)**[8]  
- Uses eight Fama‑French factors 1963–2024 and finds that **hyperbolic decay fits mechanical factors**, crowding accelerated post‑2015, and **out‑of‑sample the model over‑predicts remaining alpha (0.30 vs 0.15)**.[8]  
- Crowding predicts tail risk: in 2001–2024, **crowded reversal factors** show 1.7–1.8x higher crash probability in bottom‑decile returns, while *crowded momentum* shows relatively lower crash risk (0.38x, p=0.006).[8]  
- **Anti‑pattern:**  
  - Temptation to build a “decrowding” factor overlay (long uncrowded, short crowded) as a new alpha engine. That might sound like a new paradigm but, crucially, the inputs are the same standardized factor returns that institutions already trade via funds/ETFs; the paper itself documents over‑prediction of alpha and elevated crash risk on crowded legs.  
  - Also, while there is explicit modeling of decay and OOS calibration, there is no **retail‑replicable, net‑of‑cost, low‑turnover trading system** that passes your Filter 2 and 5.

**Factor Premiums Are Shrinking — Or Are They? (FIRE Wealth OS, 2026)**[2][12]  
- Synthesizes Asness and others: **post‑publication factor premiums are roughly 50–70% of historical magnitudes**, consistent with McLean & Pontiff’s ≈58% drop.[2][12]  
- Size has decayed the most, value has halved but remains positive over long horizons; momentum retains the largest premium, with more modest decay.[2]  
- 2020–2024 is a partial recovery for value; the article recommends underwriting future factor premiums at ~50–70% of historical estimates.[2][12]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** Encourages a “value is back” narrative, but:
  - This is **not a structural regime‑change proof**; it’s a Bayesian shrinkage argument (“expect half the historical SR”).  
  - Using it as evidence that “value structurally outperforms again” would violate your trigger condition 3 (which requires a *regime change invalidating* the “value underperforms” thesis).  
  - It would also encourage re‑engaging **Layer 2g value‑style picks** without new, robust evidence.

**Most academic stock ‘anomalies’ stopped producing useful … (2026 article)**[15]  
- Summarizes large‑scale replication: median long‑short return across anomalies falls from 0.48% per month pre‑2005 to 0.19% post‑2005, and only about **0.07% per month** in the more investable universe — essentially wiped out by costs or chance.[15]  
- When shrinkage‑adjusted, even the top performer’s post‑2005 investable return falls from 66 bps/month raw to **6 bps/month luck‑adjusted**.[15]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** This is actually *anti‑reactivation* evidence: suggests anything that looks like “classic anomaly factor” in 2020s should be assumed to have **vanishing net edge** unless proven otherwise with very careful post‑2020 OOS and multiple‑test correction.

**The ZCAPM and Large Online Datasets of Anomaly Portfolio Returns (2026 chapter)**[9]  
- Reports that **161/319 anomalies** are “clear predictors” out‑of‑sample, and that 153 factors across 93 countries survive joint modeling and OOS tests; they can be grouped into fewer clusters.[9]  
- Sound pro‑alpha at first glance, but:
  - Uses **factor portfolios**, not implementable, net‑of‑cost retail strategies.  
  - The evidence is more about **statistical predictability** than economic net returns with realistic frictions.  
- Over‑interpreting this as “lots of robust anomalies exist” can mis‑calibrate your prior and lead you back to single‑window factor mining.

**Overlapping Factor Momentum (Finance Research Letters, 2026)**[35]  
- Finds that **overlapping factor momentum** (long/short portfolios built from overlapping winner/loser factors) outperforms stock‑level overlapping momentum; factor‑level momentum explains stock‑level momentum but not the other way around.[35]  
- **Anti‑pattern:** Close cousin of your **small‑cap themed momentum** failure:
  - The effect lives in *factor space* (constructed from returns), and backtests will be inherently exposed to **multiple‑testing on factor definitions and horizons**.  
  - No evidence in snippet of **net‑of‑cost returns**, and factor momentum is likely to require non‑trivial turnover.

**Attention Factors for Statistical Arbitrage (arXiv:2510.11616, 2025)**[14]  
- Claims an **OOS Sharpe >4** on largest U.S. equities over 24 years, and a **one‑step solution Sharpe 2.3 net of transaction costs** using an Attention‑based factor model.[14]  
- **Anti‑pattern / red flag cluster:**
  - Extraordinary performance claims (Sharpe >4 OOS) are inconsistent with the broader factor‑decay literature and with the “half‑Sharpe” post‑publication discount consensus.[12][15]  
  - No explicit discussion in snippet of **multiple‑testing correction** across architectures/hyperparameters.  
  - Even if the authors apply conservative trading-cost assumptions, it almost certainly violates your turnover constraint (Filter 2) and possibly your public‑data constraint (Filter 3), as such strategies often rely on rich intraday or proprietary feeds.  
  - Pursuing this would be classic **single exceptional backtest** chasing — likely to reproduce your earlier overfit layers.

### Practical implications for your AlphaLens project

Given the absence of TRIGGER_REACTIVATION candidates and the strong factor‑decay/crowding evidence:

- Treat **new anomalies or architectures** (attention factors, diffusion/agentic portfolios, regime‑aware ML) as **candidate feature sets** for research **within a robust, multi‑window, post‑2020 OOS validation framework**, not as plug‑and‑play strategies.  
- Allocate more research bandwidth to:
  - **Robust OOS design** (multiple rolling windows, nested cross‑validation) inspired by the better‑designed factor‑decay papers.  
  - **Transaction‑cost and turnover modeling**, since most promising strategies in the recent literature live in regimes of very high turnover.  
  - **Replication of decay results on your own universe**, to calibrate realistic forward Sharpe targets before considering any reactivation.

Under your current governance rules, the literature up to 2026‑09 argues for **continued pause on capital deployment**, and for a focus on tooling and validation improvements rather than new alpha paradigms.
