The retail-limit-order paper is economically relevant to the first basket: it reports that limit orders represent 25.5% of retail orders and 30% of submitted shares, and finds economically small execution-cost differences associated with principal execution.[7] However, the available information does not establish a 15-year sample, post-2010 OOS validation, net strategy returns, sub-100% turnover, multiple-testing correction, or multi-asset coverage.[7]

The counterparty-decomposition paper is potentially useful because it distinguishes retail–retail from retail–institution interactions and separates market-order, submitted-limit-order, and executed-limit-order imbalance. Its reported continuation and reversal patterns are more structurally informative than aggregate retail-flow signals, but the Korean exchange data are not demonstrably replicable from the permitted public retail-quant data sources, and the required net-return and multiple-testing evidence is absent.[3]

The LLM disclosure papers do not meet the investment-validation standard. The “From Text to Alpha” paper reports stronger predictive performance than an NER baseline, but the available record does not document the required long sample, post-2020 multi-window OOS testing, net transaction-cost returns, public-data replication, turnover, or correction for search across specifications.[13] The taxonomy paper evaluates extraction quality and risk-profile similarity—not a tradable, net-return strategy—and therefore cannot pass the return-based filters.[2]

## Anti-pattern flags

- **Retail order-flow concentration risk:** The most interesting retail-flow evidence relies on broker or exchange datasets with trader-identity and order-handling fields that are not obviously available through CRSP, Compustat, Polygon, or FRED. This creates a direct replication problem for AlphaLens.

- **Single-market microstructure:** The counterparty-decomposition result is based on Korean-market data. Even if its mechanism is credible, it does not establish a retail-replicable, multi-asset edge across U.S. equities or other markets.[3]

- **Execution-quality rather than alpha:** “Retail limit orders” provides useful microstructure facts, but lower implementation shortfall is not equivalent to a profitable predictive strategy. The reported principal-execution effect is economically small relative to the broader marketable-versus-limit-order cost difference.[7]

- **LLM extraction mistaken for investment alpha:** The 10-K papers primarily demonstrate extraction, classification, or representation quality. Accuracy, embedding separation, and semantic similarity do not establish net tradable returns, low turnover, or robustness after factor controls.[1][2][4]

- **Potential look-ahead and model-selection risk:** The LLM alpha paper reports a favorable comparison with a recent baseline, but the available abstract does not establish frozen prompts/models, temporal train-test separation, multi-window post-2020 OOS testing, or White Reality Check/FDR/Bonferroni correction.[13]

- **Wrong filing type for the closed 8-K paradigm:** The 10-K basket is directionally distinct from the closed 8-K event-driven strategy, but no candidate yet demonstrates that the distinction produces a validated investment edge. The extraction papers should not be treated as evidence that the 8-K failure has been repaired.

- **No evidence for cross-asset overlays:** The available scan did not surface a 2024–2026 paper that simultaneously demonstrated a public-data, net-return, low-turnover commodity/FX overlay on an equity factor, with at least 15 years of data, post-2010 OOS testing, and formal multiple-testing control.

- **No evidence for post-2020 factor-decay reactivation:** The available results did not identify a qualifying 2025–2026 factor-decay study with the specified long sample, post-2020 OOS validation, net returns, public replication, and multiple-testing correction.

- **Prior failed paradigms remain unaddressed:** Nothing in the surfaced evidence repairs the known weaknesses in small-cap themed momentum, Form 4 cluster buying, tactical sector rotation, 8-K event trading, or LLM-generated value picks. In particular, none demonstrates a regime change that overturns the 2020s value-underperformance concern.
